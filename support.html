Support and safeguards

A reporting channel is only useful when it leads to thoughtful, responsible support.

What happens after a report?

In the proposed SafeSpace model, a trained counselor reviews the concern, responds with guidance and explains possible next steps. Serious or urgent cases may be referred to the Domestic Violence and Victim Support Unit (DOVVSU) of the Ghana Police Service through a formally agreed process.

Any real implementation must establish who can access reports, how consent and safeguarding duties are handled, and how students can get help if they are in immediate danger.

Privacy by design

The proposal aims to minimize the risk of identifying students. These are design intentions, not verified features of this demonstration website.

⌁

Minimal information

The planned service would not ask students to enter their names in the reporting form.

▣

Restricted access

Access to real reports should be limited to specifically authorized, trained safeguarding personnel.

🔒

Secure handling

A live system would require encryption, secure backups, access logs, independent testing and a documented retention policy.

If you are in immediate danger

Move to a safer place if possible and contact a trusted adult or appropriate local emergency service. If a school staff member is involved, consider approaching a trusted person outside that chain of authority or an appropriate child-protection service.

For schools and partners

Before launch, SafeSpace would need counselor training, a written referral protocol, clear safeguarding responsibilities, student consultation, security testing and approval from relevant education and protection authorities.
