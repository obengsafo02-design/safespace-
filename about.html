Our purpose

Why SafeSpace exists

To make it easier for students to seek help and receive guidance in a way that considers privacy, access and dignity.

The challenge

Sexual harassment and abuse can affect a student's well-being, confidence and education. Students may remain silent because they fear they will not be believed, may be blamed, or may be exposed to gossip. Reporting can feel especially difficult when the person accused has authority or influence within the school.

Digital reporting tools may also be inaccessible where students cannot use SIM cards or connect to the public internet.

How the proposed system works

The design uses equipment located within the school and existing student tablets.

Connect to school Wi-Fi

Students connect their tablet to the SafeSpace local Wi-Fi network. The proposed service does not require internet access.

Choose a service

They can open the reporting channel, request advice, or use the offline academic collaboration hub.

Receive a case code

In the proposed reporting system, a random code allows a student to return to a conversation without entering a name.

Counselor support and referral

A trained counselor reviews the concern, provides guidance and, where appropriate, follows an agreed referral process for serious cases.

Our design principles

Privacy: collect as little identifying information as possible.

Access: design for a local network without mobile data.

Care: involve trained adults and clear safeguarding procedures.

Accountability: establish appropriate access controls and referral arrangements.

What SafeSpace is not

It is not an emergency response service, a substitute for professional counseling, or a replacement for police and child-protection procedures. A working deployment would require institutional approval, security testing, trained personnel and a formal referral protocol.
